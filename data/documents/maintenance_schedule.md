# General Maintenance Schedule (Gasoline, Compact/Mid-size Sedan)

These are general industry guidelines drawn from the Car Care Council's
published service interval schedule and several major service chains
(see Sources below), organized for this project's demo dataset. **Always
defer to the specific manufacturer's owner's manual for a real vehicle**
— manufacturer intervals can differ meaningfully from these general
figures, and severe driving conditions (frequent short trips, towing,
extreme temperatures) call for shorter intervals than normal driving.

## Every 3,000–5,000 miles (roughly every 3–6 months)
- Engine oil and filter change (conventional oil). 3,000 miles is a
  conservative interval aimed at severe-service drivers; many modern
  vehicles are rated for 5,000–7,500 miles or use an oil-life monitor
  instead of a fixed interval.
- Tire rotation (commonly every 5,000–8,000 miles).
- Basic fluid top-offs and a visual brake inspection.
- Check all fluid levels (coolant, brake, power steering, washer).
- Visual inspection of belts and hoses; replace when cracked, frayed,
  glazed, or showing excessive wear.

## Every 15,000–30,000 miles
- Cabin air filter replacement (commonly every ~15,000 miles or annually).
- Engine air filter inspection (replace if dirty).
- Fuel system cleaning / transmission fluid check.
- Multi-point inspection: suspension, steering, exhaust.

## Every 20,000–30,000 miles (or with full synthetic oil, up to 12 months)
- Engine oil and filter change (full synthetic oil can extend the
  interval up to roughly 7,500–15,000 miles depending on the vehicle).
- Brake pad thickness measurement.
- Wheel alignment check.

## Every 25,000–40,000 miles
- Brake fluid flush — brake fluid is hygroscopic and absorbs moisture
  over time, which lowers its boiling point; general guidance is roughly
  every 2 years or 25,000–30,000 miles.
- Power steering fluid replacement (roughly every 3 years / 40,000 miles).

## Around 60,000 miles
- Spark plug replacement (iridium/platinum plugs often last well beyond
  this, but 60,000 miles is a common inspection/replacement point for
  standard plugs).
- Transmission fluid service.
- Coolant flush consideration (see below).
- Drive belt (serpentine) inspection/replacement.
- Timing belt inspection begins on belt-driven engines (see below — this
  is a critical, non-negotiable interval).

## 60,000–100,000 miles
- **Timing belt replacement (belt-driven engines only).** This is one of
  the most safety/cost-critical intervals on the whole schedule: on an
  interference engine, a snapped timing belt lets the pistons collide
  with the valves, destroying the engine. Manufacturers commonly specify
  60,000–100,000 miles; never exceed the owner's manual interval.
- Water pump replacement is often done alongside the timing belt as a
  preventative measure, since both require similar access.
- Coolant flush (older conventional coolant roughly every 30,000–60,000
  miles; long-life OAT coolant can extend to 100,000+ miles/around 5
  years depending on type).

## Battery
- Have the battery load-tested annually once it passes the 3-year mark.
- Typical service life is roughly 3–5 years depending on climate and
  usage; extreme heat shortens battery life.

## Tires
- Rotate roughly every 5,000–8,000 miles.
- Replace when tread depth reaches 1.6 mm (2/32", the legal minimum in
  many regions) or sooner (around 3 mm) for safer wet-weather
  performance.
- Check tire pressure monthly; underinflation is a leading cause of
  premature wear and blowouts.

## A Note on "30-60-90" Schedules
Many shops describe service using a 30,000/60,000/90,000-mile milestone
schedule: routine items (oil, filters, wipers) stack up at every
milestone, while bigger-ticket items (brake fluid flush, timing belt
inspection, spark plugs) tend to concentrate around the 60,000-mile mark
and again at 90,000+.

## Seasonal Notes
- Before winter: check antifreeze concentration, battery condition,
  wiper blades, and tire tread.
- Before summer: check A/C performance, coolant level, and tire
  pressure/condition, since heat accelerates tire wear.
